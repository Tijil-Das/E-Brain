Lining both walls were towering racks of dark metal cylinders and sealed crystal tablets arranged in meticulous rows, while in the center of the hall stood a raised circular table bearing a three-dimensional relief map of the entire globe--showing coastlines, landmasses, and ocean basins as they had existed before the last great rise of the seas.

Before Arthur could take more than three steps inside the threshold, the sharp crack of a rifle shot echoed across the lagoon outside, followed immediately by the frantic clanging of the *Endurance's* ship's bell.

Arthur spun around and sprinted back out onto the stone landing beside Captain Reed. Emerging through the narrow basalt entrance channel from the open sea was a sleek, steel-hulled steam yacht flying no flag, its bow already swinging broadside to bring a deck-mounted gun to bear on the anchored schooner. Standing on the yacht's forward bridge with a pair of binoculars raised to his eyes was Gareth Sterling.

They followed our coal smoke across the strait, Reed growled, drawing his revolver as he ran back toward the dory. Get back into that vault, Mr. Pendelton, and seal that door behind you! My crew and I will hold them off from the schooner--they won't take the *Endurance* without a fight!

No! Arthur shouted, stopping at the water's edge as another rifle shot splintered the gunwale of the dory. If they shell the schooner in this narrow basin, you won't have room to maneuver! Look at the entrance channel--it is barely sixty feet wide!

Arthur looked back up at the towering stone lantern gallery burning silently above them, then down at the brass astrolabe still clutched tightly in his left hand. Whoever had built this outpost had not left it defenseless against intruders who entered the lagoon without permission.

With a sudden surge of clarity, Arthur raced back up the carved basalt steps into the vaulted hall, heading straight for the central circular table beneath the relief map. Just as he had suspected, set into the rim of the stone table directly facing the entrance of the basin was a second circular recess, flanked by three small levers engraved with directional glyphs matching the outer rings of the astrolabe.

Outside, the deep boom of the yacht's deck gun shook the cliffs, sending a shower of loose pebbles rattling down into the water as a shell exploded harmlessly against the basalt wall twenty yards astern of the *Endurance*.

Arthur pressed the silver-inlaid disk into the recess on the central table and pulled the outer bronze lever sharply toward himself.

Deep beneath the basalt floor, a profound, rhythmic thrum awakened, rising rapidly in pitch until the very air in the chamber vibrated. High above on the tower parapet, the pale blue flame intensified tenfold, casting a blinding beam downward into the dark water of the lagoon.

Across the basin, beneath the narrow entrance channel where Sterling's steel yacht was maneuvering to fire a second round, the black water suddenly boiled upward in a violent vortex of white foam. Two massive, submerged pylons of dark alloy rose smoothly from the seabed directly beneath the yacht's keel, lifting the steel hull halfway out of the water and wedging it fast across the narrow basalt entrance like a cork in a bottle.

The yacht's steam whistle shrieked wildly as her engines churned uselessly in the air, trapped immovable between the twin pillars of the ancient harbor barrier.

For several seconds, silence fell over the lagoon save for the hiss of escaping steam from the stranded yacht and the gentle lap of waves against the stone steps. Slowly, Arthur lifted the silver disk from the table, walked back out into the cool Arctic air, and stood beside Captain Reed on the landing as the *Endurance's* crew lowered two longboats to row across and secure the surrendered vessel.

Looking out over the quiet hexagonal basin, with the timeless blue beacon shining steadily above the ancient vaults and Julian's life's work finally vindicated, Arthur Pendelton realized that his journey had not been an escape from the past after all. It had been a crossing over a threshold--and the real work of understanding what lay inside that mountain had only just begun.
